## What a hot desk costs in Sydney

Prices vary a great deal depending on the suburb, the size of the operator and how much of the building you are really paying for. As a broad guide across Sydney, you can expect somewhere in these ranges:

* **Casual day passes:** roughly $40 to $85 a day at most independent spaces, with premium serviced offices in the CBD charging well above that and the Inner West and inner suburbs sitting at the lower end.
* **Weekly rates:** usually the equivalent of three to four day passes, so you get two or three days effectively thrown in.
* **Monthly hot desk memberships:** anywhere from about $150 to $1,100 a month across Sydney, with the average landing somewhere close to $690, depending on the location and how much access you get.
* **Dedicated desks:** commonly $550 to $750 and up, because the desk is yours permanently and nobody else sits at it.

For context, our own rates at Desk and Studio are published openly on the [desks page](/desks/): $55 for a desk for a day, $165 for a desk for a week and $650 for a desk for a month, all including GST. We would rather you knew before you enquired.

## Why the headline rate is not the real cost

The number on the pricing page is only half the story. Two spaces can advertise the same daily rate and cost you wildly different amounts once you actually work there. The things that quietly add up are:

1. **Bills and add-ons.** Some operators charge separately for printing, meeting rooms, lockers or after-hours access. Ask what is genuinely included before you compare two prices.
2. **Minimum terms.** A cheap monthly rate attached to a twelve month contract is not cheap if your work is seasonal or project based.
3. **Commute.** A desk that is twenty minutes closer to home is a desk you will actually use. An underused membership is the most expensive kind.
4. **Coffee and lunch.** A space with a proper kitchenette saves you a surprising amount over a month of bought coffees.
5. **What you would otherwise hire.** If the space includes a meeting room, or a studio you can shoot in, that replaces something you were paying for elsewhere.

## Day, week or month: which one is actually cheapest for you

The honest answer depends entirely on how many days a week you want to be out of the house. Here is the simple way to work it out.

1. **Count your realistic days, not your hopeful days.** Be honest. If you say four days and manage two, a monthly membership costs you double per day of use.
2. **Do the per-day maths.** Divide each rate by the number of days you will actually turn up. On our rates, a week at $165 breaks even against day passes at three days. A month at $650 breaks even at around twelve days, which is roughly three days a week.
3. **Weigh flexibility against savings.** Day passes cost more per day, but you pay nothing in a quiet month. That is worth real money to freelancers with lumpy workloads.
4. **Try before you commit.** Book a day or two first. A space can look perfect online and feel wrong the moment you sit down in it.
5. **Think about the desk you want, not just the price.** A designated desk you can leave set up saves you the daily ritual of unpacking, which matters more than most people expect.

## What should be included in the price

Before you compare rates, make sure you are comparing the same thing. A desk worth paying for generally comes with:

* **A proper desk and chair**, ideally with lockable drawers so you are not carrying everything home each night.
* **Fast, reliable internet.** Ours runs on NBN wifi at 100Mbps down and 40Mbps up, which matters if you are moving large image or video files.
* **Lockable storage** for gear, which is a genuine consideration if you shoot for a living.
* **Printing**, a kitchenette, and all bills rolled into the one number.
* **A meeting room** you can book when a client wants to see you in person.
* **Access that suits your hours.** Our dedicated Deskies have 24 hour access, and hot Deskies can arrange after hours by appointment.
* **Cleaning**, which sounds trivial until you work somewhere that does not do it.
